AI vs. Admissions: The Uni Application Shift

By Cherielle Team • 09/26/2026

Many different fields and sectors have experienced drastic changes due to the sudden and accelerated growth of AI technology, and the education industry is no exception: in fact, it is one of the most heavily impacted sectors. The effects of AI are especially prominent in the college application space, where many students spend years preparing while dedicating their entire educational career to become what is considered a “stellar applicant.” Students, teachers, and colleges are all using AI in different ways during the college application process. This makes it imperative for people to understand how AI has changed the system, but many people still remain uninformed.

All groups involved in college applications feel the pressure of AI, but it’s not unfair to say that the group facing the most change are students. Generative AI has made an already terrifying situation for students even more difficult to navigate with things such as changing essay rules, faulty AI-detectors, and fear of getting automatically rejected due to sorting systems powered by AI. Additionally, students don't receive proper guidance on how to deal with the issues that present themselves in an already stressful time, which can create further anxiety. Keeping this in mind, it is important to acknowledge that the majority of students sending in applications do use AI in some way during the process, though not the way that people may assume. According to a survey conducted by Collegewise, around 66% of students said that they feel that using AI will negatively affect their application but the same study found that 100% of students admitted to using AI on their applications. However, these students didn’t just open up ChatGPT or Claude and ask for an exceptional essay to impress the admissions officer; applicants mainly used AI to discover and explore schools that better fit them, brainstorm essay topics, fix their grammar and flow, and even study for standardized tests. But there are plenty of risks associated with using AI in this manner, such as AI making up information or providing wrong details or answers, and a significant issue is that AI can sound generic and that makes an essay that could have been exciting sound unremarkable. This is why many colleges emphasize that a student should write in their own voice. Developing a unique style of writing can help an applicant seem unique and interesting which makes them stand out in a sea of other students where everyone seems so similar. For students, AI can be extremely helpful for certain things but good results can only be achieved through mindful and responsible use, otherwise it will just further hinder an application.

However, students are not the only ones utilizing AI in the admission process because many colleges themselves are also starting to implement different systems for a variety of purposes. Colleges are mainly using AI to screen and review a student’s application. They are saying this has many benefits but one that is particularly interesting is that it is being used so that admission results can be released earlier. This can be helpful to students by allowing them to better plan their decisions after results are out because they will have more time. For example, Virginia Tech was the first major public university in the United States to publicly come out about their AI use with their essay reader that utilizes machine learning. The college justifies this by saying that AI is more consistent in reviewing compared to humans and that it will make reviewing thousands of applications faster therefore they will be able to get decisions out earlier by around a month. Virginia Tech isn’t the only school trying to integrate AI into their application systems though, many other schools like Caltech, Penn State, UCLA, Georgia Tech, UNC, and more all use AI for different but similar purposes. UCLA uses Turnitin for plagiarism checks, Penn State also uses Turnitin and they rejected 48 essays that were marked for plagiarism, UNC uses machine learning to assess grammar and writing style, and Caltech uses AI to check how authentic an application is. All of these schools, as well as many others, continue to emphasize that AI can just be used as a tool to streamline the admissions process, and that admissions officers will be the ones to review applications and make the final decision.

Artificial Intelligence has forever changed education and university enrollment but it is not completely unnavigable. Students and colleges need to reach a consensus on what acceptable AI use looks like for both sides and lay down important guidelines and rules. It might feel like applicants and schools are on different sides but it is in everyone’s best interest to work together to find a solution acceptable to both groups to prevent misunderstandings that could lead to unfortunate scenarios like a student being misjudged. It is crucial that all parties are transparent with one another and have integrity in their activities because otherwise it could make a grueling process even more intimidating when it instead has the opportunity to improve.
